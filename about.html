About

CompSci v1.1

A compressed, mastery-based path from zero programming experience to a job-ready software engineer, with as much senior-level technical depth as structured study can actually build. Nights and weekends. Not optimized for ease. Locked 21 August 2026. Not teaching until you are on C0.1 — which is now.

Design principles

  1. Mastery, not completion. Watching a video does not move you forward. You advance when you can explain, implement, debug, and choose when (not) to use the idea.
  2. University spine, professional muscle. CS that bootcamps skip, plus Git, review, testing, CI, deploy, observability, and brownfield that universities skip.
  3. Two gates, not one. Job-ready is pulled as far forward as the hiring bar allows. Program-complete happens after you can apply.
  4. One stack, three languages, not five. Python first. TypeScript for professional web. C for systems. SQL the whole way.
  5. AI increases productivity after you can do the work without it. Every major skill is assessed twice: closed-resource and AI-open. Gate A uses Independent.
  6. I will not claim coursework made you a senior engineer. I will mark what still requires production experience.
  7. I am the instructor. Resources are assigned. I teach, question, review, remediate. This is not a playlist.

How a lesson works

I explain the concept. I ask what you already think it is. Assigned reading is specific pages and minutes, not “watch this course.” I demonstrate one example. You do the next one out loud. Socratic questions. Exercises closed-resource first. Then an assignment. I review like a demanding mentor. Remediation if a skill drops. We advance only on demonstrated mastery.

If you try to skip a foundation, I will stop you and tell you why. If the work is poor I will say so. If the work is excellent I will make the next thing harder. I will not optimize for praise.

Ratings

Not introduced → Introduced → Developing → Competent → Proficient → Mastered. Course exit: all of that course’s skills at Competent, with at least one Proficient on the load-bearing skill, plus passing exams. Dual scores: Independent and Assisted. Graduation and Gate A use Independent unless the exercise is explicitly AI-open.

Gate A — job-ready

Build, test, and deploy P1. Write correct Python under a 45-minute clock while talking. Modify an unfamiliar repo. Git at PR quality. Relational schema and an index you can justify. A small HTTP API. Tests that catch a real bug. Debug with a hypothesis. Use AI without accepting unread output. Explain networking and HTTP at user level. Five STAR stories that are yours. Pass the eight-exam battery. Load-bearing skills Proficient: programming, debugging, SQL, Git, testing, AI evaluation.

Passing means I will say you are competitive for junior / associate SWE roles at product companies and strong startups. It does not mean FAANG L3. It does not mean we stop.

Gate B — program complete

Gate A, plus C and the machine model, a concurrency bug you can fix, OS mental model, algorithms including NP as a stop rule, P2 with a design doc, a security threat model of your own app, one HLD interview at competent junior/mid, capstone at rubric pass, three scored mocks at hire / lean-hire.

Gate C

I will not award senior-engineer-level judgment from coursework. Coursework can simulate vocabulary and checklists. It cannot replace owning a service, real on-call, or calibrated distrust of AI after you have been burned in production.

Stack

Python 3, TypeScript, C (systems only), SQL / Postgres, enough HTML/CSS to ship. Tools from week 1: Unix shell, Git, a real editor, pytest / vitest, later Docker, GitHub Actions, one cloud. Default plan 15 hours / week. Pre-gate about 665 student hours; program about 1,780.

AI rules

Closed-resource first. Assisted later, after Competent, or on work I mark AI-open. Never unread output. Independent is the gate score. Cursor, Copilot, Claude — I do not care which brand. I care about the switch. In C0 and most of C1 the switch is off.
