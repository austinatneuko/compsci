Objectives

  • Explain a transaction as a bundle that commits or rolls back together.
  • Use BEGIN / COMMIT / ROLLBACK, or your driver's equivalent, on a money-shaped example.
  • Name isolation only at a user level: dirty reads as a story, not as a PhD.
  • Know that a crashed request mid-write is why this exists.

Teaching

A transaction groups statements. All of them happen or none of them do. Atomicity, in the slogan. You transfer $10 from A to B: subtract and add. If the process dies after the subtract, you do not want a world where the money vanished. COMMIT makes the bundle visible. ROLLBACK undoes it. A connection that dies usually rolls back. Do not assume. Verify.

Two writes that must both happen share a transaction. Two independent writes do not. A crash mid-transfer is the story. Isolation anomalies wait for C13. Today you need atomicity you can demonstrate. Isolation is what do concurrent transactions see. At user level I want one story: two requests updating the same row, and what your app should do. One retries, or you use a constraint, or you lock. Full isolation levels are C13. Do not pretend you mastered SERIALIZABLE because you read a table.

Your API should not leave half a user created. If two inserts must both succeed, they share a transaction. If they are independent, they do not. Transactions are not a blanket you throw over main.

ORMs hide this badly when you do not know it exists. That is why ORM is after SQL, and after this module. ORMs hide this. That is why they wait.
