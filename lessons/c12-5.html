Vectors and matrices

C12 Applied Probability and Linear Algebra

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Treat a vector as a list of numbers with operations (add, scale, dot).
  • Treat a matrix as a linear map, or as data (rows as records) — say which.
  • Multiply a small matrix and a vector by hand.
  • Refuse metaphor soup (“vectors are arrows in the space of meaning”) until C12.7.

Teaching

Linear algebra you will use: vectors, matrices, multiply, a least-squares line (C12.6), a taste of embeddings (C12.7). 3Blue1Brown for pictures, a written text for computation. You will multiply by hand so a library call is not magic.

Dot product as “weighted sum” and as “similarity” when vectors are normalized — we will be careful. Not all arrows are embeddings.

Probability and linear algebra you will use: Bayes on a debugging story, expectation and tails, a few distributions, vectors and matrices, least squares, a dry sentence about embeddings so you cannot be sold nonsense. Not a math degree. Not a deep learning course. Written work plus a small simulation. The exit is a posterior, a transformation you can interpret, and that dry sentence.

Add, scale, dot. A matrix is a map or a table of rows — say which. Multiply by hand first. Similarity talk waits until C12.7 and stays dry. A library call after the hand product, not before.

Numbers, then English. A simulation you do not Photoshop. A posterior on a story I wrote, not a medical textbook only. Embeddings without metaphor soup. Least squares as a minimization you can compute. If a vendor cannot say the loss and the data, you now have a reason to walk away. That was the point of the last module.
