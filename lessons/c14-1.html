C14 Security Fundamentals

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Write a threat model: assets, actors, entry points, what you will not defend.
  • Use it on a P1 feature, not on an abstract internet.
  • Refuse a model that is a list of category names with no assets.
  • This is the exit's first verb.

Teaching

Security fundamentals start with what you are protecting and who you are not. A threat model is a short document: assets (credentials, other users' orders, admin powers), actors (anonymous, user, admin, someone on the path), entry points (HTTP, logs, backups), assumptions you are willing to write down. Honesty is required. A matrix of category names with no assets is a poster.

You will threat-model a P1 feature. Name the asset, the actor, and what a failure looks like for a user. That is the model. We stay on systems you own. We do not touch other people's systems. Ever.

C8 already shipped an OWASP-lite checklist. C14 is the deeper pass after the gate: model first, then primitives, then classes of web failure, then secrets, then a sharper look at auth, then a PR review exam.

Security after the gate is a threat model, primitives as contracts, classes of web failure as broken boundaries, secrets and supply chain, a sharper look at auth, and a PR review exam. You will not invent crypto. You will not touch systems you do not own. The exit is a model of a feature, two real bugs in a PR, and a TLS paragraph that is not wrong. C8 was the checklist. This is the defense.

Assets, actors, entry points, assumptions you will write down. A category list with no assets is a poster. Stay on systems you own. C8 was a checklist. This is the document I reject until it is specific to a P1 feature.
