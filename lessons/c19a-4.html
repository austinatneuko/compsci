Objectives

  • Write five STAR stories that are yours (prior career counts).
  • Include a miss or a conflict that is not a humblebrag.
  • Answer without inventing a software story you did not live.
  • Keep each story under two minutes, with a result.

Teaching

Behavioral loops hire people. Your prior career is an advantage if you tell it as decisions, not as a job description. Situation, Task, Action, Result. The Action is you, singular, not “we.” The Result is specific. A miss you learned from is worth more than a fake hero story.

I will poke: “what did you do,” “what would you do differently,” “tell me about a time you were wrong.” If P1 is your only software story, we will use it honestly and use prior work for collaboration and conflict.

Do not let a model invent stories. I will ask follow-ups only a person who lived it can answer.

Professional readiness before the gate is evidence and manners, not a grind that replaces P1. Resume that is not a lie. GitHub that runs. Algorithm and debugging reps in interview voice. STAR stories that are yours, including prior career. Explain P1 without slides. A tracker, and no applications until I say the work is evidence.
