Code review (Google eng-practices)

C4 Software Construction

Locked. Because the prerequisite course is not mastered. You are on C0.1. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Review a PR using Google's eng-practices as a baseline, not as scripture.
  • Separate correctness, design, tests, and nits.
  • Say what you would block on versus what you would note.
  • Accept review on your own code without collapsing or lawyering.

Teaching

Code review is the number-one industry-reported junior gap. We practice it as a skill, not as a vibe. You will review my intentionally mixed PRs: real bugs, real design smells, and a few nits. You will also receive review that is blunt. This is not good enough is the working relationship, not a mood.

Google's engineering practices on review are a good default. The review is for the change, the tests, the clarity, not for proving the reviewer is clever. Block on correctness and security and missing tests. Do not block on a rename you prefer unless the name is a lie. Prefix nits. If everything is a blocker, nothing is.

A review comment has three parts. What is wrong, why it matters, what better looks like. This is bad is not a review. Have you considered rewriting the module is not a review of this PR unless the module is the change.

You will not rubber-stamp. An LGTM on a PR that fails its own spec is a fail for the reviewer. That is the standard I will use on the code-review exam at the gate. Rubber-stamping a PR that fails its spec is a fail for the reviewer. I will be blunt on your PRs.

Review at least three of my mixed PRs in writing. Respond to my review of yours. Fix, or argue with evidence. No AI drafting your review until I mark one AI-open, and then you must catch the model's false nits. A generated review full of style comments that miss the hole is how you fail Gate A.6 later.

C4 is the course where other people have to live with your code. Specs, ADTs, tests, PR-quality Git, review, a debugging method, API-shaped errors, a little FP taste, and AI evaluation as law. MIT 6.1020 is the gold statement of the ideas; we do the work in Python. Google engineering practices are a baseline, not scripture. Dual-track work starts here: closed first, then catch the model. If you treat this course as a checklist of tools, you will still write a 400-line main. If you treat it as contracts plus history plus review, P1 becomes possible.

A review comment has three parts: what is wrong, why it matters, what better looks like. Block on correctness, tests, and safety. Prefix nits. Rubber-stamping a PR that fails its spec is a fail for the reviewer. That is the gate exam. I will be blunt on your PRs. That is the working relationship, not a mood.
