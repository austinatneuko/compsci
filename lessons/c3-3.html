Induction and invariants

C3 Discrete Mathematics

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Write an induction that names P(n) the base, and the inductive step.
  • Refute a wrong induction, the classic "all horses are the same color" shape.
  • State a loop invariant and use it to argue a small program.
  • Refuse a proof that only checks examples.

Teaching

Induction is the proof tool you will actually use on algorithms and on loops. You name a statement P(n). You prove P(base). You prove P(k) implies P(k+1) or a strong-induction variant. The failure mode is assuming what you wanted, or stepping from 0 to 1 in a way that does not generalize, the horses.

A loop invariant is P that is true before the loop and after each iteration, and that plus "the loop finished" gives you the postcondition. C4 specs meet C3 here. You will prove an invariant on a program you wrote in C2. that is the course exit's first sentence.

I will give you a fake induction to refute. Spotting a fake is as useful as writing a real one. Interviews and papers are full of fakes.

Name P(n) prove the base, prove the step. The horses proof fails the step from 0 to 1 in a way that does not generalize. A loop invariant plus termination gives the postcondition. That is the course exit's first sentence, on your C2 code, orally.

Proofs are written so I can see the claim, the setup, the argument, and the close. Examples are not a proof. A symbol pile is not a proof. Oral defense is how I catch a fluent fake. A model will give you a fluent fake. Closed.
