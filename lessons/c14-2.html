Crypto primitives

C14 Security Fundamentals

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • State what a hash, a MAC, encryption, and a signature each guarantee.
  • Know the difference between hashing a password (slow, salted) and hashing a file (fast).
  • Refuse to invent a primitive.
  • Reuse the TLS paragraph and make it still not wrong.

Teaching

Crypto primitives are contracts. A hash is a fingerprint of a blob. A MAC or signature is about authenticity. Encryption is about confidentiality. Public-key versus symmetric is about who holds what secret. You will not implement a block cipher. You will not design a protocol. You will match a job to a primitive and stop.

Password hashing is a different job from file hashing: slow on purpose, unique salt. A fast general-purpose hash of a password is a fail. C8 said this; now you can say why the jobs differ.

A short applied-crypto chapter I assign is the text. The exit includes a TLS paragraph that is not wrong — C11.6 plus this module. TLS authenticates a server (usually) and encrypts bytes. It does not authorize your user and it does not fix a bad query.

Security after the gate is a threat model, primitives as contracts, classes of web failure as broken boundaries, secrets and supply chain, a sharper look at auth, and a PR review exam. You will not invent crypto. You will not touch systems you do not own. The exit is a model of a feature, two real bugs in a PR, and a TLS paragraph that is not wrong. C8 was the checklist. This is the defense.

Match the job to the primitive and stop. Password hashing is slow on purpose. Fast file hashes are a different job. TLS paragraph v2 must still not be wrong. You will not implement a block cipher. You will not design a protocol.
