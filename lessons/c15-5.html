Transactions across nodes

C15 Distributed Systems and Data-Intensive Apps

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • State the problem: a transaction that spans nodes is a different animal.
  • Meet two-phase commit as a protocol with a blocking failure mode.
  • Prefer an outbox or a saga-shaped application pattern when I assign them.
  • Do not sprinkle distributed commit on P2 as fashion.

Teaching

Single-node transactions do not automatically cross nodes. Two-phase commit tries; it can block if the coordinator dies at the wrong time. Many product systems avoid distributed transactions and use an outbox, a saga, or they put the facts on one node. You will name the trade, not implement a coordinator.

P2 may use a queue instead. That is a choice we will write down.

Distributed systems here is DDIA, not a required lab course. Failure and time, replication, consistency words, partitioning, transactions across nodes, logs and queues, idempotency, case studies. You will operate a small worker. You will write a design I will try to break. You will have named the happy path first. Exactly-once end-to-end is usually a lie. At-least-once plus an idempotent handler is an adult sentence.

Two-phase commit can block if the coordinator dies at the wrong time. Prefer an outbox, a saga, or one node. P2 may use a queue instead. Write the two-service version of a two-table write and what you do. Do not sprinkle distributed commit as fashion.

Write the failure down before I produce it. Consistency is a word with a definition. A queue you operate beats a drawing of a log you have not run. Idempotency is how retries become safe. Case studies keep the vocabulary from becoming bingo. 6.824 is recommended watching, not a required lab sequence. The design I attack is the exit.
