Trees / BST

C2-lite Data Structures (pre-gate)

Locked. Because the prerequisite course is not mastered. You are on C0.1. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Implement a binary search tree with insert, search, and an in-order walk.
  • State the cost in terms of height, and when height is log n versus n.
  • Walk a tree recursively without losing the plot.
  • Leave balancing as a named problem, not as a homework you fake.

Teaching

A binary search tree stores keys so that left is less and right is greater. We will pick a total order and stick to it. Search walks a path, not the whole tree, if the tree is not a stick. Insert hangs a new node at a leaf position the same walk found. In-order walk emits keys sorted. That is the payoff you can test without poetry. If the keys do not come out sorted, your insert is wrong. You cannot fake that test.

A BST is a comparison policy plus two child pointers. Height is the cost. A balanced tree is logarithmic. A tree built from already-sorted keys is a linked list in costume, and every operation is linear. I will make you insert sorted data and watch the stick appear. If you cannot produce the stick on purpose, you do not yet believe height is the cost. Balancing, AVL, red-black, is real and not in C2-lite. You will name the problem so you do not lie in an interview with trees are log n after you inserted sorted ids. That honesty is part of the module.

Recursion from C1.8 is the default walk. An explicit stack is legal. Drawing a six-node tree is mandatory before you debug a rotate you are not even implementing yet. Draw the tree after inserting 5, 3, 7, 2, 4. Bring the drawing. I will ask what happens if the next key is 1, and I will wait while you draw, not while you guess.

Python does not have a BST in the standard library you should reach for in application code. dict is hashed, not ordered. bisect on a sorted list is the other logarithmic tool. We will mention it so you do not invent a tree when a sorted array was the job.

One paragraph you can say without notes: height vs n; when a BST loses to a hash; when it wins because you need an ordered walk. If the paragraph is trees are fast, we rewrite it.

C2-lite is judgment plus implementation, not a museum of types. A structure is the operations you need and the bill they send when n grows. You already use lists and dicts; now you must be able to build the ones a junior actually needs and say when a list beats a hash. Heaps and graphs-as-ADTs wait until after the gate. That is not because they are optional forever. It is because the hiring bar uses lists, hashes, and trees, and we will not pretend you implemented a heap if we did not sit with one. Every module ends with costs you can say out loud.

A BST is a comparison policy plus two child pointers. Sorted input is the stick. If you cannot produce the stick on purpose, you do not yet believe height is the cost. In-order walk is the test you cannot fake: the keys come out sorted or your insert is wrong. Balancing is named, not implemented. That honesty is part of the module.
