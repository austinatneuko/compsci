Objectives

  • Design a small JSON HTTP API: resources, verbs, error shape.
  • Keep URLs as nouns. Keep verbs in the method.
  • Document one error body and stick to it.
  • Refuse a design that is one POST /doStuff that does everything.

Teaching

REST, in the sense juniors can defend, is this. Resources have URLs. Methods mean what HTTP says. Representations are JSON in this course. Errors are consistent. We are not doing dissertation REST. We are doing APIs a teammate can call without reading your source.

A resource is a thing you can name. /orders. /orders/119. Creating is POST to the collection. Reading is GET. Replacing is PUT. Partial update is PATCH if you mean it. Deleting is DELETE. Query parameters filter. They do not hide a second RPC. If you need an RPC such as /orders/119/cancel, we will name it and live with it, honestly.

Error shape is a spec. I will pick one for the course repo, a small object with error and code, or RFC 7807. Status codes stay honest. Do not invent a parallel language of success false. Honesty means 404 is a missing noun, not a panic, unless we decided to hide existence and wrote that down.

I will reject a single do-everything POST. I will reject designs until they are honest. Design first. Code after I accept the paths. The loop is the assignment. I send a prompt you have not seen in class. Paths, methods, error examples. I reject. You revise.

Nouns in the URL, verbs in the method, one error body. If you cannot defend the errors, you do not have an API. You have a bag of handlers. Closed-resource on the first draft. No AI writing a fashionable resource tree you cannot explain.

Backend work in this program is HTTP plus a relational database plus a thin framework, in that order. FastAPI is not the syllabus. If you cannot read a raw request, predict a join, or justify an index, a green OpenAPI page is decoration. We talk to Postgres with parameters before any ORM. Transactions are how two writes become one fact. Validation dies at the edge with a 4xx that names the field. The service you build here is the skeleton of P1. Dishonest schemas get rejected until they are honest. That loop is the course.
