Graphs

C3 Discrete Mathematics

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Use graph vocabulary: directed/undirected, degree, path, cycle, connected, tree.
  • Prove a simple claim, e.g. a tree on n nodes has n-1 edges, without waving.
  • Connect to BFS/DFS you already ran in C5-lite.
  • Model a problem as a graph on purpose.

Teaching

You have already walked graphs as an algorithm student. Here they are mathematical objects. Definitions first: what is a path, when is a graph a tree, what does bipartite mean. A proof about graphs is usually induction on nodes or edges, or a walk that cannot be extended.

Modeling is the engineering skill: conflict as an edge, dependency as a directed edge, a grid as a graph you already used. If you cannot state the nodes and edges, you do not have a graph algorithm, you have a vibe.

The course exit asks you to prove a simple graph claim. We will pick one and you will defend it orally.

Nodes and edges first, then the claim. A tree's edge count is an induction you can finish. Modeling a conflict as an edge is the engineering move. If you cannot state the nodes, you do not have a graph algorithm. One oral claim, defended.

Discrete math in this program is CS 70 style: math you will use on programs and algorithms, not a symbol museum. Junior loops do not test proofs. That is why we deferred this past the gate. It is still the program. We did not delete CS. We reordered it.

Proofs are written so I can see the claim, the setup, the argument, and the close. Examples are not a proof. A symbol pile is not a proof. Oral defense is how I catch a fluent fake. A model will give you a fluent fake. Closed.

Discrete math is deferred because junior loops do not test proofs, not because proofs are optional for a complete engineer. Logic, sets, induction, counting, graphs, modular arithmetic, and proofs you will use on programs. You will prove a loop invariant on code you wrote in C2, prove a simple graph claim, and refute a wrong induction. No coding-heavy work. A fluent fake proof is a fail when I poke a hole. This course feeds C5-remainder and C12. If a loop exposes the hole during search, we schedule it sooner. We do not lower the gate.
