Resume and public work

C19a Professional Readiness A

Locked. Because the prerequisite course is not mastered. You are on C0.1. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Write a resume that is evidence, not adjectives.
  • Make GitHub a set of readable repos (P1, P0), not a graveyard of forks.
  • Remove lies, including implied years you do not have.
  • Take my markup without adding “synergy.”

Teaching

A resume for this program is a one-page (or two if you have a prior career worth a page) list of things you actually did. P1 is the lead once it exists. Prior work is welcome if it is true. “Full-stack ninja” is not a line I will send. Impact in numbers is good when the numbers are not invented.

Public work is a repo someone can clone. A README that runs. A commit history that is not one dump. I will look. Private-only work is weaker evidence; we will say what we can say.

You will not apply until I say the resume is sendable. Applying with a lie or with an empty GitHub is how you spend the search twice.

Professional readiness before the gate is evidence and manners, not a grind that replaces P1. Resume that is not a lie. GitHub that runs. Algorithm and debugging reps in interview voice. STAR stories that are yours, including prior career. Explain P1 without slides. A tracker, and no applications until I say the work is evidence. C19b continues through the search with LLD, HLD, take-homes, live review, and an open-source PR. Do not spray eighty copies on day one of C0.

Evidence is a repo someone can clone and a line you can defend in a STAR. Adjectives are not lines. Implied years you do not have are lies. I mark up v1. You do not apply until I say it is sendable. A model-written resume full of verbs you cannot explain will die in the first screen.

Work

Exercises are listed so you can see the shape of the work. They are not assigned. Do not start them. No solutions on this site.

  3. No AI-generated resume full of verbs you cannot defend in a STAR.
