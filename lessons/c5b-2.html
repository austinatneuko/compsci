Divide and conquer

C5-remainder Algorithms I (remainder)

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Recognize divide-and-conquer: split, recurse, combine.
  • Implement one, mergesort you may already know, or closest-pair-lite, or inversions.
  • State the recurrence and the cost.
  • Know when D&C is fashion, tiny n, vs real.

Teaching

Divide and conquer is the algorithm shape behind mergesort, many tree algorithms, and a class of interview problems that are not "use a hash." You split the input, solve smaller copies, combine. The combine is often the interesting bill.

We will implement one non-trivial combine. If you only have mergesort from a video, we pick inversions or something I name so the combine is yours.

The combine is often the bill. Mergesort you may know; we will pick a combine you have not copied. Recurrence plus tests. Tiny n does not need this shape. Say when you would just scan.

C5-lite taught you to narrate a medium. This course teaches you to classify a new problem, implement when it is the kind we solve, state complexity, and say whether a polynomial exact algorithm is likely. If it is not, you name an alternative: restrict, approximate, or exponential on small n. That sentence is engineering, not a meme about hardness.

Remainder is also a stop rule. Some problems do not have a clever polynomial exact algorithm you are going to invent on a Tuesday. Implementations still have tests. Proofs still have structure. Conceptual exam on NP catches slogans.
