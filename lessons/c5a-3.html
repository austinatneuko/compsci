Two pointers and windows

C5-lite Algorithms for interviews (pre-gate)

Locked. Because the prerequisite course is not mastered. You are on C0.1. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Use two pointers on a sorted array or on a string window.
  • Know when a sliding window applies: contiguous, monotone shrink or grow.
  • Avoid O(n^2) rescans when a window would do.
  • State the invariant of the window in one sentence.

Teaching

Two pointers is a pattern, not a religion. Sorted two-sum, reversing in place, partitioning: two indexes that move with a reason. If the reason is I saw it in a video, you do not have the pattern.

A sliding window maintains a contiguous region that is the candidate answer. You expand when you need more, shrink when you violated a constraint, and the invariant is this window is always the smallest or largest or valid thing I can defend. If you cannot say the invariant, you will off-by-one the shrink. A window has an invariant you can say: this region is always a valid candidate I can defend. If you cannot say it, you will shrink wrong.

Not every n-squared problem is a window. If the region is not contiguous, stop forcing it. I will mix in a problem that looks like a window and is not. Not every quadratic problem is a window. I will mix in a trap that looks like one and is not.

Assigned problems: at least one two-pointer, one window, one trap. Narrate the invariant. Closed-resource. Timed quiz. No AI.
