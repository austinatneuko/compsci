Objectives

  • Walk a binary tree: pre, in, post, and a BFS level walk.
  • Write recursive and, once, explicit-stack versions of the same walk.
  • Use a BST's order when the tree is a BST.
  • Narrate the call stack on a four-node tree.

Teaching

C2 gave you BST insert and search. C5-lite uses trees as interview objects. Given a node with left and right, compute a height, a path, a validity check. Recursion is the default. I will still make you draw the stack from C1.8 and C2.5. Walks first: pre, in, post, and a queue for levels. Recursion is default. Paper stack on four nodes is required.

BFS on a tree is a queue of nodes. C2.3 earning rent. Level-order problems are queues. Lowest-common-ancestor and validate-BST are recursive jobs with a contract on what you return. Validate-BST is a contract on what you return, not a vibe about looking sorted at the root.

Do not memorize fourteen tree problems. Master the walks and one validate. Transfer.

Draw before you type. If the paper walk is wrong, the code walk is a guess.

C5-lite is interview-shaped algorithms you can narrate, not a second theory course. Asymptotics as sentences. Arrays and hashes. Two pointers and windows. Trees. BFS and DFS. One greedy and one DP you can tell as state, recurrence, base, order. NP and MST wait. Timed narration is the skill the gate coding exam will use.
