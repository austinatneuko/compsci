TLS and trust

C11 Computer Networking

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Explain TLS as authenticating the server (usually) and encrypting the bytes.
  • Say what a certificate chain is for, at user level.
  • Know what TLS does not do (authorize your user, fix SQL injection).
  • Prepare the C14 paragraph so it is not wrong.

Teaching

TLS is the handshake and record layer under HTTPS. The browser checks a certificate chain to a trust store. That is authenticity of the server (ideally). Then keys, then encryption. It does not mean the HTML is safe. It does not mean the person at the keyboard is who they typed. Those are different jobs (C8, C14).

Expired certs, wrong host names, someone intercepting with a corporate root — stories you should recognize. You will not invent a handshake.

Networking is how HTTP stops being magic. Layers, sockets, TCP, a short DNS and routing, HTTP as bytes, TLS as a job, a packet lab. You will write a small TCP client and server and explain a slow API with the network, not the framework. C-syslit was the seatbelt. This is the course. You will not become a network engineer. You will read a capture without drowning.

Authenticity of the server, then encryption. Not authorization of your user. Not a fix for a bad query. Expired certs and wrong names are stories you should recognize. You will not invent a handshake. The paragraph must survive C14.

A capture is evidence. A slogan about the cloud is not. Client and server you wrote are evidence. Kurose and Ross selected, Wireshark labs I assign. Optional CS144 is depth, not an excuse to skip the small socket work. Explain slowness with hops and windows and loss, then maybe the handler.
