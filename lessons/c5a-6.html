Objectives

  • Solve one greedy problem you can prove-by-exchange at a hand-waving junior level.
  • Solve one DP problem you can narrate as state, recurrence, base, order.
  • Refuse to call every loop DP.
  • Leave NP and DP depth for after the gate.

Teaching

Greedy: make the locally prescribed choice, earliest finish, biggest first, depends, and show it does not burn the optimum. I want one problem you can tell as a story, not a catalog of greeds. If you cannot say why greedy is safe, it is a heuristic and you should say that. Greedy needs a reason it does not burn the optimum, even a junior-level one.

DP: name the state, what is the subproblem, the recurrence, how it uses smaller, the base, and the order you fill, or the memo. One problem: coin change or knapsack-tiny or climb-stairs. I pick. You will narrate before you write the table. Deep DP and knapsack variants wait for C5-remainder. DP needs a state that is not the whole answer. If the state space explodes, say so. Calling every loop DP is a tell.

The point is fluency in the words so a junior loop that throws one medium DP does not become a silent panic. The point is not to become an algorithms specialist before you can ship P1. We do one of each well. Depth waits.

One greedy, one DP, both narrated, both tested on small fixtures. Closed-resource. I pick the problems so you cannot shop solutions. No AI. No I watched a 3-hour DP video as a substitute.
