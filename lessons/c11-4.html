Routing/DNS (short)

C11 Computer Networking

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Explain DNS as a query for records, with a cache, that can fail.
  • Explain routing at a user level: packets are forwarded toward an address.
  • Keep this short.
  • Use dig or an equivalent I assign, carefully.

Teaching

DNS is a distributed database of names to records (A/AAAA, MX, CNAME). Your laptop asks a resolver; caches lie for TTL seconds (and sometimes longer when they misbehave). NXDOMAIN is a fact. A wrong record is how the whole URL walk goes to the wrong box.

Routing: each router looks at the destination IP and picks a next hop. You will not design BGP. You will know that “cannot reach” might be a route, a firewall, or a dead process on an open route — three different checks.

Networking is how HTTP stops being magic. Layers, sockets, TCP, a short DNS and routing, HTTP as bytes, TLS as a job, a packet lab. You will write a small TCP client and server and explain a slow API with the network, not the framework. C-syslit was the seatbelt. This is the course. You will not become a network engineer. You will read a capture without drowning.

A resolver, records, TTL, caches that can lie. NXDOMAIN is a fact. Routing is hop-by-hop toward an IP. Cannot reach might be a route, a firewall, or a dead process on an open route. Three checks, not one guess. Short module on purpose.

A capture is evidence. A slogan about the cloud is not. Client and server you wrote are evidence. Kurose and Ross selected, Wireshark labs I assign. Optional CS144 is depth, not an excuse to skip the small socket work. Explain slowness with hops and windows and loss, then maybe the handler.
