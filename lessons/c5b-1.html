C5-remainder Algorithms I (remainder)

Locked. Until Gate A. Systems depth and remaining CS wait until you can apply. You may read so the catalog is a real map. Do not start the exercises or the assigned reading until this lesson is current. Ratings do not move from a URL.

Objectives

  • Write a recurrence for a recursive algorithm.
  • Solve a simple one, unfold or Master theorem case I assign.
  • Connect the solution to a running-time sentence.
  • Refuse a recurrence that does not match the code.

Teaching

C5-lite narrated "this is n log n." Remainder asks you where that came from. A recurrence is T(n) = a T(n/b) + f(n) or a variant. You must derive it from the code: how many subproblems, what size, what work outside. Solving is algebra plus the Master theorem cases I will teach, or unfolding a simple one by hand.

Wrong recurrences come from ignoring the work outside the recursive call, or from assuming perfect split when the code does not split. I will show you a lie and you will catch it.

Derive from the code: how many subproblems, what size, what work outside. A recurrence that ignores the outside work is a lie. Unfold one by hand. Master theorem cases I teach, not a table you worship. Match the code or throw the recurrence away.

C5-lite taught you to narrate a medium. This course teaches you to classify a new problem, implement when it is the kind we solve, state complexity, and say whether a polynomial exact algorithm is likely. If it is not, you name an alternative: restrict, approximate, or exponential on small n. That sentence is engineering, not a meme about hardness.

Remainder is also a stop rule. Some problems do not have a clever polynomial exact algorithm you are going to invent on a Tuesday. Implementations still have tests. Proofs still have structure. Conceptual exam on NP catches slogans.
